MS-MOLAR — Introduction
Marketing copy, written 2026-08-09. Everything below describes features that are live today at https://ms-molar.vercel.app. Anything not yet shipped is confined to the final section and clearly labelled as upcoming — please keep that separation if you edit this file, because an ad that promises a feature the site doesn't have costs more trust than the feature would have earned.

What MS-MOLAR is
MS-MOLAR is an online exam-practice platform for Vietnamese secondary and high school students (THCS and THPT).

You open it, pick a real exam, answer it under a real clock, and get your score the moment you submit — with the correct answer next to your answer, question by question. Every attempt is kept, so the platform can show you what you keep getting wrong instead of just what you scored today.

It runs in the browser. There is nothing to install, and it is built to be usable on a mid-range Android phone on an unreliable connection — which is what most students actually study on.

Why it exists
Practising past exams is the single highest-value thing a student can do before a test, and it is also the most annoying thing to organise. The papers live in scattered PDFs, group chats and photocopies. Marking them means flipping to an answer key at the back and hoping you copied your own answers correctly. And the part that actually makes practice work — noticing that you fail the same kind of question every time — is the part nobody has the patience to do by hand.

MS-MOLAR exists to remove all of that friction and leave only the practice:

The exam bank is alive, not a static archive. Anyone can contribute an exam paper, so the bank grows with each new school year instead of ageing out.
Marking is instant and honest. You never grade yourself, and you never wait.
Your history is the product. Each attempt is stored and charted, so "where am I weak?" becomes a page you open, not a feeling you guess at.
The project deliberately does one job and does it properly. It is not a social network, not a video course, not a chat app with a study theme.

What you can do on it
Find an exam worth doing. Browse the published bank and narrow it with filters — subject, grade, difficulty, and sorting by newest, oldest or hardest. Each exam shows its duration and difficulty up front, so you can pick a paper that fits the 30 minutes you actually have.

Sit it like the real thing. A countdown timer runs while you work, you can flag questions to come back to, and submitting is one action. If you run over time, the result says so plainly rather than silently discarding your work.

Get scored the moment you submit. The result screen gives you the score and the time taken, then a question-by-question breakdown: your choice, the correct answer, and which ones you skipped. Question types that can't be auto-scored (short answer, essay) are stored and shown to you rather than being quietly dropped or guessed at.

Look back at everything you've done. The History page keeps every submitted attempt, filterable by score range, and any result can be exported as a PDF to save or share — useful for showing a tutor or a parent what you've been doing.

See your weak spots, not just your scores. The Analytics dashboard charts correct versus incorrect answers by subject, and which subjects you practise most — over the past week, the past month, or all time. That comparison is the point: the subject you score worst in is often the subject you avoid practising.

Turn your own exam paper into a practice test — with AI doing the typing. Upload a PDF or a photo of an exam and the AI reads it and extracts the questions and options for you. Two rules keep this trustworthy: you review and edit everything before it goes live, and answers always come from your answer file — the AI never invents them. You can also enter an exam manually if you prefer. Once published, your exam joins the shared bank for everyone.

Keep the bank clean. Students can rate exams and report bad ones, and there is a moderation path for removing papers that don't belong. A contributed bank only stays useful if bad entries can be taken out.

Study in your language. The whole interface switches between English and Vietnamese with one tap, and the choice sticks.

Built with the boring things done right
These rarely make an ad, but they are the reasons the site is pleasant to use:

Phone-first, genuinely. Bottom navigation within thumb reach, tap targets sized for fingers, no horizontal scrolling, and layouts measured on a 360px screen rather than assumed to work.
Accessible. Built to WCAG 2.2 AA — keyboard navigation, screen-reader labels, real contrast ratios, and motion that respects the "reduce motion" setting on your device.
Your data is yours. Access rules are enforced in the database itself, not merely hidden in the interface, so one student's attempts and drafts are not reachable by another.
Fast where you are. Hosted in Singapore, close to Vietnamese users, so pages arrive quickly on ordinary mobile data.
Who it's for
Students (grades 6–12) who want to practise for a test this week and see progress over a term.
Teachers and tutors who want a class to sit the same paper and be able to see the marked results as PDFs.
Anyone with a stack of old exam papers that would be more useful as a practice test than as a folder on a hard drive.
Coming next
Adaptive practice: using what your attempts reveal about your weak skills to choose what you should practise next, so revision time lands where it actually moves your score. This is in active development and is not live yet — don't advertise it as available.
